How does Thicket name what I found?

It shows the species people have recorded on iNaturalist near you, at this time of year, so you can pick the right one.

Why is my find a photo, not a cut-out?

When a photo has no clear subject, Thicket keeps it whole as a print. To cut it out yourself, open the find, tap ⋯, then Cut it out.

What do the gold marks under a name mean?

How often your find’s species had been recorded within 25 km of where you found it, out of all records there. A star is 1 in 10,000 or rarer, two stars 1 in 100,000 or rarer, and a flag means no one had recorded it there before.

What can I search for?

Type what you’re after in Around, the way you’d say it: “venomous snakes”, “edible mushrooms”, “what’s flowering now”, “birds I haven’t found”. The answers come only from what people have recorded within 25 km.

What does the red mark mean?

It’s on anything that can seriously hurt you: venomous snakes, spiders and scorpions, plants that poison or burn skin, and deadly or poisonous mushrooms. The species page says why. Thicket only ever warns, so no mark never means safe. Keep your distance from wild animals, and never eat or touch a wild plant or mushroom because an app named it.

Can I post my finds to iNaturalist?

Yes. On a find, tap Post with iNaturalist. Thicket opens the share sheet with the photo, its date and place ready for iNaturalist’s app, and copies the species name for you to paste.

Where are my finds kept?

Only on your phone. See Privacy.

Species, photos and sounds come from iNaturalist and the people who share there. Thicket isn’t made by iNaturalist.
