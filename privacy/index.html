Privacy

Last updated November 24, 2026

Thicket has no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no tracking. Your finds, with their photos, stickers, places and dates, are kept only on your phone. Nothing is sent to us, because Thicket has no server.

What leaves your phone, and why

  • Species near you. To show what lives around you or around a find, Thicket asks iNaturalist’s public service. It sends a location rounded to about 1 km. These requests go straight from your phone to iNaturalist, and iNaturalist’s privacy policy covers them.
  • Photos and sounds of species are loaded from iNaturalist the same way.
  • Searching in Around sends the name of what you’re looking for, such as “owls”, to iNaturalist, to find that group of living things. Words like “venomous” or “near me” are read on your phone. On an iPhone with Apple Intelligence, Apple’s on-device model may read a search too; that stays on the phone.
  • Place names and maps come from Apple Maps, under Apple’s privacy policy.
  • Bringing in your iNaturalist sightings sends the username you type to iNaturalist, to fetch your public observations.
  • Posting a find happens only when you choose to. Thicket hands a copy of the photo, with its date and place, to iNaturalist’s app through the share sheet, and copies the species name for you to paste.

What Thicket asks to use

  • Camera, only while you’re spotting something.
  • Location, while the app is open, to show what lives near you and remember where you found something. You can turn it off in Settings; finds then save without a place.
  • Photos you pick are read on your phone: Thicket cuts out the subject and reads the date and place inside the photo. It never sees the rest of your library.

Deleting

Removing a find deletes it from your phone. Deleting the app deletes everything it kept. If your phone backs up to iCloud, your finds are in that backup.

Children

Thicket doesn’t collect personal information from anyone, children included.

Crash reports

If you share analytics with app developers in your iPhone’s settings, Apple may pass on crash reports without your name or personal details.

Changes

If this changes, the date at the top will too.
